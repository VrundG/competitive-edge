# NCCEE competition preparation: source review and implementation brief

Reviewed October 4, 2026. Prepared for the proposed NCCEE Economic Education Equity Initiative student interface. This is a product research brief, not an official NCCEE policy document. Research and synthesis were assisted by AI; unresolved source conflicts are preserved below.

## Method and authority

Started with [NCCEE's competition page](https://www.nccee.org/financial-literacy-programs/academic-competitions/) and [resource directory](https://www.nccee.org/financial-literacy-programs/financial-education-resources/). Followed their links to the North Carolina competition portals, CEE, EconEdLink, and NCDPI. Read national rules PDFs, state schedules and rule images, standards documents, toolkit pages, sample resources, and the supplied BFF proposal. Inspected NCCEE's published HTML, stylesheet, and page appearance. No third-party prep marketplace is used as an authority.

Authority depends on the claim: NCCEE/NC portals for state administration; season-specific CEE rules for national competition; CEE/Jump$tart and NCDPI for learning frameworks. A resource appearing on an official website does not guarantee its dates or answer key remain current. No assessment form was submitted or student identity entered during research.

## What the product must do

Students should be able to choose a competition, find the official practice experience, study the relevant subject areas, assemble a temporary study sheet, and understand how to join a team. School outreach should explain the coach-led process and offer a usable draft/checklist. The first screen should expose the two preparation paths immediately.

The interface is a preparation companion. Official registration, student access codes, scored competition rounds, and eligibility decisions remain with NCCEE and its existing platforms. The prototype does not invent official questions, scores, exam timers, registration acceptance, or a student leaderboard.

## North Carolina competitions

### Economics Challenge

Sources: [Welcome](https://econchallenge.unl.edu/North%20Carolina/Welcome), [How it works](https://econchallenge.unl.edu/North%20Carolina/HowItWorks), [rules](https://econchallenge.unl.edu/North%20Carolina/Rules), [schedule](https://econchallenge.unl.edu/North%20Carolina/Schedule), [practice](https://econchallenge.unl.edu/North%20Carolina/Practice).

High-school teams have 3–4 students and a teacher/coach. Teachers register, receive administrator approval, create teams, and distribute individual access codes. The NC platform describes single-use assessment codes and one competitive assessment per student per school year; fall participation therefore affects spring eligibility. Team scoring uses the top three individual results.

The sample practice page identifies its examples as David Ricardo level and says its purpose is familiarization with the platform, not a substitute for study. It asks for first and last name and shows a score at completion. This is an external official practice experience, not a question bank supplied to this prototype.

The published schedule is the 2025–26 cycle: fall November 17–December 19, spring January 20–March 27, and an April 20, 2026 state final. Do not display these as upcoming. The schedule describes eight teams per division and a two-team-per-school/division limit, with exceptions at NCCEE's discretion. It also contains a financial-family case description that appears inconsistent with an economics event; obtain coordinator clarification before encoding that state-final format.

### Personal Finance Challenge

Sources: [Welcome](https://financechallenge.unl.edu/North%20Carolina/Welcome), [How it works](https://financechallenge.unl.edu/North%20Carolina/HowItWorks), [rules](https://financechallenge.unl.edu/North%20Carolina/Rules), [schedule](https://financechallenge.unl.edu/North%20Carolina/Schedule), [practice](https://financechallenge.unl.edu/North%20Carolina/Practice).

NC offers middle- and high-school participation; national eligibility is a separate high-school pathway. Teams have 3–4 students. The welcome page says NCCEE covers participation costs, but this should not become a blanket promise covering travel, all competitions, or future seasons.

The NC rules image specifies 40 questions in 45 minutes, ten points per correct answer, and a top-three team sum. CEE's general national program page describes a 30-question early round. Neither should become a universal practice-exam specification. The NC sample practice page uses high-school examples, asks for a name, and explicitly frames the sample as platform familiarization.

The NC schedule uses the 2025–26 online windows above and April 24, 2026 for its state final. The welcome page retains different dates. Treat these as historical pages, not a verified 2026–27 calendar.

## National rules: season and stage matter

Read [2026 NEC rules](https://www.councilforeconed.org/wp-content/uploads/2026-NEC-Rules-and-Eligibility-1.pdf) and [2026 NPFC rules](https://www.councilforeconed.org/wp-content/uploads/2026-NPFC-Rules-and-Eligibility-1.pdf). Both define 3–4-person teams, organization/coach requirements, conduct, and restrictions on outside assistance. Their national rounds do not automatically describe NC state rounds.

NEC divides entrants into David Ricardo and Adam Smith. Prior participation, course level, college credit, and homeschool provisions matter; a simple “beginner/advanced” toggle cannot determine eligibility. The 2026 national online round uses 45 questions/45 minutes. National finals include critical thinking and whiteboard quiz rounds. Link the full rules rather than hiding exceptions in a short summary.

NPFC's 2026 national case round allocates 75 minutes of preparation, eight minutes of presentation, and seven minutes of questions. The later quiz stage has stage-specific rules. Neither event permits treating AI or online notes as allowed competition assistance. Lodging/meal provisions do not imply covered transportation.

[CEE's NPFC program page](https://www.councilforeconed.org/programs/for-students/national-personal-finance-challenge/) says registration opens in January 2027. An evergreen toolkit “open” banner is not stronger evidence. No verified 2026–27 NC schedule or 2027 full rules were located in the reviewed paths. This is an evidence gap, not proof that dates have not been announced elsewhere.

## Learning standards and scope

[CEE economics standards, third edition](https://www.councilforeconed.org/wp-content/uploads/National-Content-Standards-in-K%E2%80%9312-Economics-3rd-Edition.pdf) were revised in 2025. The framework has 18 standards and 260 benchmarks across elementary, middle, and high school. The old 20-standard organization is not the current edition. It combines knowledge with application and includes technology, behavior, contemporary monetary institutions, and distributional issues. Product navigation can group preparation into microeconomics, macroeconomics, and international economics, but that grouping is our navigation choice, not an official exhaustive exam blueprint. Foundations and explanation/application belong alongside practice questions.

[2021 CEE/Jump$tart personal finance standards](https://www.councilforeconed.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/2021-National-Standards-for-Personal-Financial-Education.pdf) cover six areas: earning income, spending, saving, investing, managing credit, and managing risk. Expectations are cumulative through grades 4, 8, and 12; students may need earlier foundations. High-school outcomes include comparing compensation and education choices, budgeting and consumer decisions, savings products, real returns and investment costs, borrowing terms and credit reports, insurance and fraud. Application matters: compare, calculate, evaluate, and explain. The six study areas are not a required teaching sequence or six equal exam weights.

The [NCDPI standards directory](https://www.dpi.nc.gov/districts-schools/classroom-resources/office-teaching-and-learning/standard-course-study/social-studies/standard-course-study-supporting-resources) links the [EPF standards](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1kyJEzK65V2vlaJyoOBWAYLzTUUSOk16f7Z955thcKy0/edit) and [2021 unpacking document](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1jx_x-pMzFmhmLXX1JW4hH1YaEM6gzhIdgkcqv23YQ60/edit). The course contains five strands: economics; income and education; money and credit management; financial planning; critical consumerism. The accessible standards copy carries a March 11, 2026 date; that alone does not establish a new adoption. Unpacking examples are instructional suggestions, not an exhaustive curriculum or summative test bank. NCCEE's 2020 EPF resource guide references draft standards and belongs in supplemental planning, not current rules authority.

Suggested topic correspondence, not an official crosswalk: economics preparation relates to EPF.E; earning to EPF.IE; spending/saving/credit/risk to EPF.MCM; investing to EPF.FP; consumer protection/fraud to EPF.CC. The interface links the frameworks without claiming a validated lesson-by-lesson alignment.

## Retirements, contradictions, and material uncertainties

1. [ReadyAssessments](https://econedlink.org/readyassessments/) was retired May 16, 2026. Exclude it as an active practice destination even when older official pages still link it.
2. Current NC 2026–27 schedules remain unverified in reviewed sources. Label the season gap and link official schedules; no countdown.
3. NC PF rule image (40 questions) and CEE general description (30 questions) differ. Scope may explain some difference, but it was not resolved. No local simulated “official” timer.
4. The NC economics schedule includes a finance-family case description. Preserve uncertainty; do not encode a state-final case workflow as fact.
5. NCCEE's competition page retains a NYC economics-final location while 2026 national documents identify Atlanta. Do not promote an obsolete location.
6. [Toolkit quiz-bowl samples](https://npfc.councilforeconed.org/__trashed/) remain downloadable, but the 2024 answer key contains date-sensitive and potentially misleading statements. For example, it equates a no-load fund with having no fees and supplies a fixed answer to a current-officeholder question. These samples are not included as validated on-site questions. They need subject-matter review before reuse. The live PDFs were read, not merely detected by link status.
7. The supplied BFF proposal is evidence of proposed outreach and partnership ideas, not proof of current statewide operations or a completed impact evaluation. Do not publish its personal phone numbers. The user's 129K+/40-county equity claim is not independently substantiated by this document; NCCEE's organization-wide impact counts cannot verify it.
8. A reachable source page is not proof that its complete third-party quiz can be played without an account. Research verified landing pages and linked resources, not every downstream authenticated activity.

## NCCEE visual identity and source availability

Inspected the actual [NCCEE competition page](https://www.nccee.org/financial-literacy-programs/academic-competitions/) and [published theme stylesheet](https://www.nccee.org/wp-content/themes/nccee/style.css?ver=4.0). The theme names https://github.com/dev-3ve/nccee/; the public repository API returned 404. Open-source availability/license is therefore unverified. Re-create the visible structure with new code; do not claim to have cloned an open-source repository.

Observed tokens: white page; NCCEE blue #0067a5; green #7bae3c; body gray #4d4d4d; Montserrat headings and buttons; Open Sans body/navigation. Rounded blue and green buttons use the theme's gradients. Header places the actual logo left, utility actions above the main navigation. Interior pages use a breadcrumb, blue heading, generous white space, and competition photography. Avoid the superseded navy/gold branding, artificial dashboard metrics, oversized decorative slogans, and invented organization marks.

## Implementation and review criteria

- Faithful NCCEE shell with a clearly identified preparation area; private prototype status in footer.
- Economics and finance paths, useful official practice destinations, searchable topic library, temporary study sheet, school-start checklist and editable outreach draft.
- Public browsing without a new login; explicit page-session lifetime for the temporary study sheet; print/export for preparation and low-connectivity use. External activities retain their own access requirements.
- Semantic headings, labeled controls, keyboard focus, readable contrast, mobile reflow, and no color-only status indicators.
- Dates/source links travel with time-sensitive claims. No claim that self-marked study completion predicts competition scores.
- Before a public institutional rollout: NCCEE must resolve the season/rules conflicts, decide integration/ownership and ongoing editorial responsibility, and supply any question bank intended for direct delivery. That future rollout does not block a reviewable private prototype.

## Critical review

Scope checkpoint: an official-source preparation companion is answerable; “all resources” cannot honestly mean every historic file or authenticated assessment. Coverage centers on NCCEE's two requested competition pathways and the standards/resources those pathways reference.

Evidence checkpoint: official sources disagree and contain retired links. Their authority was not used to erase conflicts. Published standards are learning frameworks, not proof of exact exam coverage. Equity benefit is a design objective, not a measured outcome.

Final research checkpoint: no critical unresolved issue blocks a source-linked private preparation prototype. Unverified current dates, exact state-round mechanics, third-party account requirements, and sample-answer quality block stronger claims and test-bank recreation. These limits remain visible in the product and this brief.
